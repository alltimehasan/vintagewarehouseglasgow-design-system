Actions

Button

Live preview, 960px layout, follows the theme switch.Open in a new tab

One primary button per view, for the thing the page is for; everything else is outline or a link. Labels are verbs in sentence case ("Add to basket"); the style sets them uppercase.

<a class="vwg-btn vwg-btn-primary" href="/shop">Shop online</a>
<button class="vwg-btn vwg-btn-outline" type="button">Get directions</button>
<button class="vwg-btn vwg-btn-link" type="button">View all bags</button>
  • vwg-btn-primary is an ink fill; vwg-btn-brick the accent fill, for the hero's call to action or checkout; vwg-btn-outline and vwg-btn-link for secondary actions.
  • On band use vwg-btn-inverse (filled) and vwg-btn-ghost (outlined); never the primary, which disappears.
  • vwg-btn-sm inside product cards; vwg-btn-block on phones at checkout.
  • Hover swaps to brick (or to ink on the brick button). Focus shows focus-ring. Disabled is 45% and not clickable, with a label that says why ("Sold out").
  • From JavaScript: VWG.Button({ label, variant, size, href, onClick }).

Live preview, 960px layout, follows the theme switch.Open in a new tab

One or two uppercase words of status, as a pill. Every product carries Vintage or New; stock and sold-out states come from the shop.

<span class="vwg-badge vwg-badge-new">New</span>

<span class="vwg-badge vwg-badge-sold">Sold out</span>
<span class="vwg-badge">Mixed sizes</span>
  • Tones: vintage (brick on brick-soft), new (blue on blue-soft), stock, sold (muted), danger (payment failed), and none for neutral facts like sizes.
  • The word carries the meaning; the colour only repeats it.
  • Tables, filters and lists only. On a product photo and on the invoice the Stamp does this job.
  • From JavaScript: VWG.Badge({ label, tone }).

Stamp

Live preview, 960px layout, follows the theme switch.Open in a new tab

A rubber-stamp label: one or two uppercase words inside a double line, tilted a little, as if stamped on a bale label. It goes on product photos (Vintage, New, Sold out) and on the invoice (Paid). Its companion is the swing-tag price, vwg-tag.

<span class="vwg-stamp">Vintage</span>
<span class="vwg-stamp vwg-stamp-new">New</span>
<span class="vwg-stamp vwg-stamp-stock">Paid</span>
<span class="vwg-stamp vwg-stamp-sold vwg-stamp-lg">Sold out</span>

<p class="vwg-price"><span class="vwg-tag">£60.00</span> <small>UK delivery only</small></p>
  • Tones: brick by default (Vintage), vwg-stamp-new (blue), vwg-stamp-stock (Paid), vwg-stamp-sold (danger). The word carries the meaning; the colour repeats it.
  • vwg-stamp-lg is the big one, tilted 12°, across a sold-out photo with vwg-card-stamp-lg. On a photo the small stamp sits top left with vwg-card-stamp.
  • The stamp has a surface-raised ground so it reads on any photo in both themes. Never put one on band.
  • The Badge pill stays for tables, filters and lists; a tilted stamp in a table row looks like a mistake.
  • The price tag: brick on brick-soft with a punched hole and a pointed end, 32px tall, tabular numerals. Product cards and the basket only; invoice lines and totals stay plain.
  • From JavaScript: VWG.Stamp({ label, tone, size }).

SectionHeading

Live preview, 960px layout, follows the theme switch.Open in a new tab

Every section opens the same way: a script lead-in in brick, the uppercase heading, a short thick-thin rule, and an optional one-line intro. The pattern is the logo's: script over bold caps.

<div class="vwg-section-heading">
  <span class="vwg-script" aria-hidden="true">shop online</span>
  <h2 id="shop">Bags of vintage and new stock</h2>
  <div class="vwg-rule" aria-hidden="true"></div>
  <p>Six bags, packed by hand in Glasgow. UK delivery only.</p>
</div>
  • The lead-in is one to three lowercase words and is decorative (aria-hidden); the h2 carries the meaning.
  • The rule is 3px over 1px, 48px wide, like a line in a ledger; it is vwg-rule and needs no content.
  • On band the rule and intro switch to on-band and on-band-muted by themselves.
  • From JavaScript: VWG.SectionHeading({ lead, title, intro }).

Field

Live preview, 960px layout, follows the theme switch.Open in a new tab

A label, a control, and a hint that turns into the error. Used on the contact form and at checkout.

<div class="vwg-field">
  <label for="email">Email</label>
  <input class="vwg-input" id="email" name="email" type="email" autocomplete="email" aria-describedby="email-hint" required>
  <p class="vwg-field-hint" id="email-hint">We only use it for your order confirmation and invoice.</p>
</div>
  • Labels are the eyebrow style and always visible; placeholders never replace them.
  • Inputs are 48px tall with a line-strong border, surface-raised fill and radius-sm; focus turns the border ink and shows focus-ring.
  • Error: add vwg-field-error to the wrapper, set aria-invalid="true", and write the fix in the hint ("Enter an email address like name@example.com"). The hint is danger; the word does the work, not the colour.
  • Stack fields in a vwg-form (16px gaps, 560px wide); the submit is a primary button, "Send message" or "Pay now".
  • From JavaScript: VWG.Field({ id, label, type, hint, error, multiline }).

A brick band of uppercase words with discs between them, like the lettering on a shop awning. It runs under the hero on the homepage, and may run above the footer on other pages; nowhere else.

<ul class="vwg-strip">
  <li>Vintage</li>
  <li>New</li>
  <li>Sold by the bag</li>
  <li>UK delivery only</li>
  <li>Glasgow</li>
</ul>
  • Five words or short phrases at most, in on-brick on brick; the discs are the logo's disc, small.
  • It is static. No scrolling marquee, in either theme.
  • Wraps to two lines on phones; the discs come with the items.
  • The strip is the one place brick is used as a full-width fill.

Live preview, 960px layout, follows the theme switch.Open in a new tab

The invoice the shop generates for every online order, so nobody writes one by hand. Titled "Online order invoice", numbered in sequence with four digits, on a surface-raised sheet with the logo top left. The company is VAT registered, so this is a VAT invoice.

  • Number: VWG.invoiceNumber(n) gives "0001"; the counter only ever goes up, and the number appears in the order email subject too.
  • Head: the black disc at 72px, then the title, number, date and the Paid stamp (vwg-stamp-stock), over the thick-thin rule.
  • Parties: From is Vintage Warehouse Glasgow, then the company name R and BM Logistics Limited, the registered office (Bay E, Barclay Curle Complex, South Street, Glasgow G14 0BX), the company number SC769949 and the VAT number. To is the customer and the UK delivery address.
  • Lines: one per bag with its weight, quantity and price, all VAT inclusive; delivery as its own line. Totals right-aligned with tabular numerals: subtotal, delivery, the grand total in the price style over the thick-thin rule, then "Includes VAT at 20%" in vwg-vat.
  • Foot: payment method and date, "All prices include VAT", and a one-line thank you.
  • The same markup prints to PDF for the order email; keep it to one page.
  • The VAT number is still to come from the client; the preview shows the slot. Nothing else on the sheet is a placeholder except the customer and the lines.
