Button

Live preview, 960px layout, follows the theme switch.Open in a new tab

One primary button per view, for the thing the page is for; everything else is outline or a link. Labels are verbs in sentence case ("Add to basket"); the style sets them uppercase.

<a class="vwg-btn vwg-btn-primary" href="/shop">Shop online</a>
<button class="vwg-btn vwg-btn-outline" type="button">Get directions</button>
<button class="vwg-btn vwg-btn-link" type="button">View all bags</button>
  • vwg-btn-primary is an ink fill; vwg-btn-brick the accent fill, for the hero's call to action or checkout; vwg-btn-outline and vwg-btn-link for secondary actions.
  • On band use vwg-btn-inverse (filled) and vwg-btn-ghost (outlined); never the primary, which disappears.
  • vwg-btn-sm inside product cards; vwg-btn-block on phones at checkout.
  • Hover swaps to brick (or to ink on the brick button). Focus shows focus-ring. Disabled is 45% and not clickable, with a label that says why ("Sold out").
  • From JavaScript: VWG.Button({ label, variant, size, href, onClick }).

Live preview, 960px layout, follows the theme switch.Open in a new tab

A rubber-stamp label: one or two uppercase words inside a double line, tilted a little, as if stamped on a bale label. It goes on product photos (Vintage, New, Sold out) and on the invoice (Paid). Its companion is the swing-tag price, vwg-tag.

<span class="vwg-stamp">Vintage</span>
<span class="vwg-stamp vwg-stamp-new">New</span>
<span class="vwg-stamp vwg-stamp-stock">Paid</span>
<span class="vwg-stamp vwg-stamp-sold vwg-stamp-lg">Sold out</span>

<p class="vwg-price"><span class="vwg-tag">£60.00</span> <small>UK delivery only</small></p>
  • Tones: brick by default (Vintage), vwg-stamp-new (blue), vwg-stamp-stock (Paid), vwg-stamp-sold (danger). The word carries the meaning; the colour repeats it.
  • vwg-stamp-lg is the big one, tilted 12°, across a sold-out photo with vwg-card-stamp-lg. On a photo the small stamp sits top left with vwg-card-stamp.
  • The stamp has a surface-raised ground so it reads on any photo in both themes. Never put one on band.
  • The Badge pill stays for tables, filters and lists; a tilted stamp in a table row looks like a mistake.
  • The price tag: brick on brick-soft with a punched hole and a pointed end, 36px tall, the height of the small button beside it, tabular numerals. Product cards and the basket only; invoice lines and totals stay plain.
  • From JavaScript: VWG.Stamp({ label, tone, size }).

Notice

Live preview, 960px layout, follows the theme switch.Open in a new tab

One sentence beside its colour, for what just happened: added to the basket, message sent, payment not completed, a fact worth knowing. It sits at the top of the page content, inside the container, never as a floating toast.

<div class="vwg-notice vwg-notice-stock" role="status">
  <p>Added to your basket. <a href="/basket">View basket</a></p>
  <button class="vwg-notice-close" type="button" aria-label="Dismiss">
    <svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.75" stroke-linecap="round" aria-hidden="true"><path d="M6 6l12 12M18 6L6 18"/></svg>
  </button>
</div>
<div class="vwg-notice vwg-notice-danger" role="alert"><p>Payment was not completed. Your basket is unchanged.</p></div>
  • Tones: none (a line-strong border on surface-raised) for plain news, vwg-notice-stock for done and paid, vwg-notice-danger for an error or a failed payment, vwg-notice-info (the container blue) for a fact such as the delivery charge. The sentence carries the meaning; the colour repeats it.
  • role="status" for news, role="alert" for an error, so a screen reader announces it.
  • The close button is optional and removes the notice, nothing more. VWG.notices() wires every close button on the page with one listener; VWG.Notice({ tone, text, link, dismissible }) builds one in the browser.
  • Square corners, a 1px border in the tone's colour on its soft tint, no shadow, no icon. One notice at a time; the next page load clears it.
  • A field's own error stays in Field: vwg-field-error on the wrapper or aria-invalid="true" on the input turn the border danger and the hint says the fix.

Invoice

Live preview, 960px layout, follows the theme switch.Open in a new tab

The invoice the shop generates for every online order, so nobody writes one by hand. Titled "Online order invoice", numbered in sequence with four digits, on a surface-raised sheet with the logo top left. The company is VAT registered, so this is a VAT invoice.

  • Number: VWG.invoiceNumber(n) gives "0001"; the counter only ever goes up, and the number appears in the order email subject too.
  • Head: the black disc at 72px, then the title, number, date and the Paid stamp (vwg-stamp-stock), over the thick-thin rule.
  • Parties: From is Vintage Warehouse Glasgow, then the company name R and BM Logistics Limited, the registered office (Bay E, Barclay Curle Complex, South Street, Glasgow G14 0BX), the company number SC769949 and the VAT number GB 522 8334 04. To is the customer and the UK delivery address.
  • Lines: one per bag with its weight, quantity and price, all VAT inclusive; delivery as its own line. Totals right-aligned with tabular numerals: subtotal, delivery, the grand total in the price style over the thick-thin rule, then "Includes VAT at 20%" in vwg-vat.
  • Foot: payment method and date, "All prices include VAT", and a one-line thank you.
  • The same markup prints to PDF for the order email; keep it to one page.
  • Every company detail on the sheet is real and confirmed by the client; only the customer and the lines are placeholders.
